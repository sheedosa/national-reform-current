Identity and direction

The National Reform Movement is an independent Libyan national initiative with a reformist political, social and intellectual outlook. It starts from the belief that reform is a shared national responsibility, and that building the state begins with reforming society, deepening awareness and strengthening a culture of citizenship.

The Movement is not built on glorifying individuals, nor on siding with any tribe, region or particular system of government. The interest of the country and the citizen is the basis of its positions.

The priority of social and political reform

The Movement holds that social and political reform is a national priority. The Libyan crisis will not be resolved simply by changing governments or replacing individuals; it requires real reform of political culture, social conduct and the performance of state institutions.

Social reform improves the chances of political stability, and political reform provides the legal and institutional environment needed to build a just and stable society.

Core principles and values

  1. Citizens first

    The dignity, rights, freedoms and standard of living of citizens are the fundamental purpose of national work.

  2. National unity

    Libya is one homeland for all its people, without discrimination, exclusion or marginalisation.

  3. Conciliation and reconciliation

    Promoting a culture of tolerance and rapprochement, and resolving disputes through dialogue and justice, far from revenge and score-settling.

  4. Justice and equality

    Equal opportunities, rights and duties for all citizens, and a fair distribution of wealth and development across the regions.

  5. Rule of law

    No one is above the law, and there is no immunity for corruption or the violation of rights.

  6. Fighting corruption

    Protecting public money, entrenching transparency and accountability, and rejecting the use of power for private gain.

  7. Respect for difference

    Belief in pluralism, and rejection of accusations of treason, exclusion and any monopoly on truth.

  8. Independent national decision-making

    Putting Libya’s interest first and rejecting dependency, tutelage and interference that undermine the state’s sovereignty.

Political foundations

The Movement’s political outlook rests on the Libyan people being the source of authority, with the inherent right to choose their system of government and those who manage their affairs.

  • Abiding by the will of the people through free and fair elections.
  • Prioritising the renewal of legitimacy through parliamentary elections.
  • Supporting a popular referendum on the constitution to settle the constitutional framework and form of government.
  • Strengthening fair regional representation through balanced legislative institutions, including a senate.
  • Supporting the unification of military and security institutions under the authority of the state.
  • Guaranteeing the peaceful transfer of power and the separation of powers.
  • Rejecting any monopoly on political decisions, or tying Libya’s future to one person, family or group.

Social foundations

The Movement sees a cohesive society as the foundation of a stable state, and so gives particular weight to:

  • Spreading a culture of reconciliation, tolerance and coexistence.
  • Strengthening the role of family and education in building awareness.
  • Respecting Libya’s social and cultural components.
  • Supporting the role of young people and women in public life.
  • Rejecting hate speech, incitement and regionalism.
  • Encouraging social responsibility and volunteer work.
  • Addressing social grievances through justice and the law.

The approach to reform

The Movement adopts peaceful, gradual and realistic reform, built on dialogue, consensus and public participation, far from violence and the imposition of beliefs.

It does not claim to hold complete solutions or a monopoly on being right. Ideas are open to debate and development, and objective criticism and continuous review are necessities of reform work.

Political realism does not mean giving up principles, just as holding to principles does not mean ignoring reality.

Independence and openness

The Movement is independent in its decisions and direction. It has no organisational ties to any party or political entity, and does not claim to represent any side or group.

It is open to dialogue and cooperation with every national figure, initiative and institution that shares the principles of reform, reconciliation, justice and the rule of law, without requiring complete agreement in views and beliefs.

The ethics of national work

In its words and conduct the Movement commits to honesty, objectivity and respect for opposing views. It rejects the political sanctification of individuals, media disinformation, and the exploitation of social affiliations for political gain.

It believes national work is a duty and a responsibility, not a route to prestige or personal interest.

The national goal

The Movement seeks to contribute to building a unified, stable, just and sovereign Libyan state, founded on law and institutions, where rights and freedoms are protected and social justice and balanced development are achieved.

It does not see itself as a substitute for the people or the institutions of the state, but as part of the national effort for reform, rapprochement and reconciliation.
